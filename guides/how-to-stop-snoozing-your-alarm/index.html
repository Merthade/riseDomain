How to Stop Snoozing Your Alarm and Actually Get Up

The alarm goes off, your thumb finds the snooze button before your brain is awake, and nine minutes later you do it again. Here is how to break the loop.

Quick answer: Snoozing sticks because the moment after the alarm has no plan, so going back to sleep wins by default. Give that moment a job: put the phone out of reach, decide the night before what the first five minutes are, and use an alarm that leads straight into a short timed session, like Rise, instead of back to the lock screen.

Why you keep hitting snooze

Right after waking, the brain is in a state called sleep inertia: slower reaction times, foggy decisions, a strong pull back to sleep. That fog lasts anywhere from a few minutes to half an hour. It is exactly the window in which the snooze button asks you to make a decision, and a foggy brain picks the option that requires nothing.

Snoozing also does not buy you real rest. Nine minutes is too short to get back into deep sleep, so what you collect is a series of interrupted light naps. Most people feel worse after three snoozes than they would have felt getting up on the first alarm.

What actually helps

  1. Move the phone. Charge it across the room. Walking to it does half the work of getting up.
  2. Pick the first five minutes the night before. Read ten pages, stretch, write one line, sit with a coffee. A plan you made yesterday beats a decision you have to make now.
  3. Make the first step tiny. "Go for a run" loses to the snooze button. "Five minutes of anything" usually wins.
  4. Keep the wake time steady. The same time on most days, weekends within an hour or so, makes the alarm land in lighter sleep more often.
  5. Get light early. Open the curtains or step outside. Light is the strongest signal your body clock has.

Give the alarm a next step

  2. When it rings and you stop it, Rise opens straight onto the wake-up screen with a focus timer, not onto your notifications.
  3. Pick a length, 5 minutes is enough on a hard morning, and start. The session runs on your Lock Screen as a Live Activity, so you can put the phone down.
  4. Finish it and your streak grows by a day. That small win is what you are trading the snooze for.

Should you remove snooze completely?

For some people, yes. If you snooze by reflex, a single alarm with no snooze forces the decision once. For others a single snooze is a buffer they actually use. The useful test is simple: if you snooze more than once on most days, the snooze is not helping you wake up, it is postponing the decision.
